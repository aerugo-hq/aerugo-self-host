# Architecture

The architecture in words, for anyone (and any agent) who can't look at the diagram. The diagram is
`aerugo-architecture.drawio` beside this file, generated by `generate_architecture.py` (run it from the repository's
root; edit it when the architecture changes, so the two never disagree). Each section here is one of its ten tabs.
The plans that build it: `docs/ENTERPRISE_PLAN.md` (the top level), `docs/ORG_INTELLIGENCE_PLAN.md` (its engineering
and data pack), `docs/PRODUCTION_REVIEW.md` (what comes first).

## Words, as this project uses them

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Source** | A system the company already has: Confluence, Jira, Teams, GitHub, a warehouse, Control-M. Always the system of record. |
| **Connector** | Reads one kind of source, read-only, with a service account whose access the company approved. |
| **Knowledge unit** | One piece of knowledge, typed: a fact, a procedure, a policy, a contact, a definition, a decision, an answer, a lesson. |
| **Evidence** | Where a piece of knowledge or a link came from: a page, a message, a file and line, a log, a person. Every claim has some. |
| **Evidence tier** | How it's known: `confirmed` by its owner, `observed` in a log or a conversation, `declared` in code or configuration, `documented`, or `inferred` by a model. |
| **Perspective** | A different version of the same knowledge held by another team or person, kept with who holds it and why, never overwritten. |
| **Owner** | The team or role responsible for a piece of knowledge: they confirm it, correct it, and review it on its schedule. |
| **Scope** | Who a piece of knowledge applies to: teams, roles, locations. Answers use the asker's scope. |
| **Atlas** | The graph of how things connect: systems, repos, jobs, tables, topics, dashboards, people, and the links between them, with evidence. |
| **Playbook** | A senior's checklist as steps the agent follows: a migration assessment, an impact analysis, a procedure, an onboarding path. |
| **Pack** | The playbooks, templates, question sets, and connectors for a family of scenarios (incidents, onboarding, engineering and data). |
| **Gap** | Something people ask that it can't answer well; routed to an expert, whose answer becomes knowledge. |
| **Question set** | Real questions with their right answers, per team and role: the tests every change to answering is held to. |
| **Pointer mode** | Keeping pointers, permissions, small facts, and embeddings, but no text: passages are fetched live at answer time and discarded. |

## 1. The big picture

Aerugo is the company's living memory. On the left, where knowledge lives today: documents (Confluence, SharePoint,
Drive), tickets (Jira, ServiceNow), conversations (Teams, Slack, meetings), code (GitHub), data (the EDW, BigQuery,
catalogs), jobs (Control-M, Airflow), people (the directory), and operations (monitoring, incidents). In the middle,
Aerugo's parts: the **knowledge core** (what the company knows), the **Atlas** (how everything connects), the
**architect agent and playbooks** (thinking in steps, like a senior), **answers and guidance**, and, on everything,
permissions from each source, evidence for every claim, an audit trail, and no monitoring of people. On the right,
where people meet it: the web app, Teams and Slack, phones and handhelds (stores and distribution centers), the IDE and
pull requests, and APIs and agents. A loop runs back from people to Aerugo: they confirm, correct, and ask, and it learns.

## 2. Components, layer by layer

Bottom to top. Built today is marked (built); everything else is to build.

- **Connectors and capture:** documents (Confluence, Drive, SharePoint, Notion: built), tickets and chat (Jira,
  Slack: built), Teams capture, meetings, and handovers, code (GitHub, at thousands of repos: built, to scale),
  warehouses, catalogs (built), and usage logs, schedulers and ETL (Control-M, Airflow).
- **Stores:** documents and their index in Postgres with pgvector (built), knowledge units, the Atlas graph, usage,
  permissions in OpenFGA (built), credentials in OpenBao or a cloud secret manager (built).
- **Pipelines** (the worker): indexing (built), extraction of knowledge units, consolidation (merge, supersede,
  conflicts), analyzers (code, SQL lineage, usage, jobs), profiles and systems, and the learning loop (feedback, gaps,
  freshness).
- **Services:** answers (search and Ask: built), the architect agent and playbooks, incident mode and onboarding
  paths, migrations, the change agent (pull requests), the review queue and gaps (built, to extend).
- **Surfaces:** the web app (built), Teams and Slack bots, the frontline mobile view, an IDE extension and a
  pull-request bot, the MCP server, APIs, and SDKs (built).
- **Across every layer:** identity (single sign-on), permissions (row-level security and OpenFGA), the model gateway
  (approved models, budgets), audit and privacy, the job queue with leases, observability (all built), sensitive-topic
  filters and question sets (to build).

## 3. What's stored (data residency)

The sources stay the system of record; Aerugo never becomes a second Confluence. What it keeps is chosen per source:

| Mode | Keeps | Doesn't keep | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Index** (today) | Pointers and metadata, permissions, small derived facts, embeddings, text passages | | Best answers, fastest, works when a source is down; a protected copy of text exists |
| **B. Pointer** | Pointers and metadata, permissions, small derived facts, embeddings | Text | No second copy of content; slower; depends on the source being up; embeddings still reveal meaning, so protect them |
| **C. Live only** | Pointers and metadata, permissions | Facts, embeddings, text | Almost nothing stored; weakest answers, slowest, no map |

In pointer mode, a question runs: the person asks; candidates are found from pointers, embeddings, and facts; the
person's permission is checked in the source; only the passages needed are fetched live; the answer is composed with
citations; the text is discarded. The map can't be pointer-only: "who uses this table", "what feeds this report" are
worked out ahead of time and kept as small facts with pointers to their evidence, carrying their sources' permissions.
Everything kept stays inside the company's own cloud project and region.

## 4. How a question gets answered

1. **Ask**, in Teams, the web app, a phone, or the IDE.
2. **Who's asking**: sign-in, and their team, role, and location from the directory.
3. **Find**: knowledge units first, then documents and the Atlas.
4. **Filter**: only what this person may see, enforced in the database itself.
5. **Fetch**: in pointer mode, the passages needed, live from the source.
6. **Think**: a playbook's steps, with the company's approved model.
7. **Check**: every claim against its evidence; what isn't supported is dropped.
8. **Answer**: citations, confidence, and both sides when teams disagree.
9. **Learn**: "this is wrong", or "ask an expert"; it gets better.

Example: a store associate asks what to do when a customer slips near the entrance. The answer is that store's
incident procedure as a checklist, who to call by role (the manager on duty, the district manager, loss prevention),
the form, and what not to do, citing the procedure's page and its owner.

## 5. How it learns

A loop: **capture** (documents, opted-in chats, meetings, handovers) → **propose** (draft knowledge with its evidence)
→ **confirm or correct** (the owner decides; corrections are kept) → **consolidate** (merge duplicates, supersede old,
flag conflicts) → **answer** (people use it in their own context) → **notice gaps** (unanswered questions, stale or
conflicting knowledge) → back to capture. In the middle: tests gate every change (per-team question sets with right
answers). Gaps go to experts; with their consent, their answers become knowledge. Every piece of knowledge has a review
schedule; stale pieces go back to their owners. It doesn't rewrite itself: its knowledge, confirmations, and playbooks
grow, under test.

## 6. Capture

Sources of what isn't written down: a Teams channel its owners opted in; "Save to Aerugo" on any message; an opted-in
Slack channel; meeting transcripts the organizer opted in; a handover interview when someone leaves; asking an expert
when an answer is missing. Everything passes the **privacy filters** (only opted-in places; "don't capture" marks
honored; direct messages never, by default; HR, medical, and legal matters excluded; personal data excluded), becomes
**proposed knowledge** with the messages it came from, and reaches the knowledge core only when **its owner confirms or
corrects it**. Teams' rules shape this: a channel's owners can consent to a bot reading all its messages, but group-chat
delivery to bots is unreliable and live notifications aren't available for private and shared channels, so capture
rests on opted-in standard channels and the save action, never on watching chats.

## 7. The engineering and data pack

Sources: GitHub (repos, Terraform, conventions), the EDW (metadata, query logs), Control-M (jobs, order, scripts),
Airflow and Composer (DAGs), dbt and Dataform (models), catalogs (Knowledge Catalog, Dataplex). **Analyzers** turn
them into facts without AI: repo facts, SQL lineage, usage, the job graph, naming and Terraform conventions. Those
facts become the **Atlas**; the **architect agent** runs the migration playbook over it; the outputs are a migration
assessment (waves by dependency, ranked by usage, retiring what's unused), lineage diagrams, a step-by-step plan
(schema, Terraform, catalog metadata, validation), and pull requests people review and merge. The question it answers:
"this EDW cube needs to move to EDP; how do we do it?"

## 8. Security and trust

A person signs in through the company's identity provider; Aerugo then checks permissions mirrored from each source
(row-level security in the database, and OpenFGA), reaches sources only with read-only service accounts whose secrets
it holds only as references, uses only the company's approved models through the gateway with budgets, and records
every read and action in the audit trail. Sources are written only by reviewed pull requests in repos that opted in.
Never: ranking people by their questions or activity; capturing a channel nobody opted in; answering from something
the asker can't see; merging a change by itself.

## 9. Where it runs

Self-hosted in the company's own cloud project, its region pinned: Kubernetes (GKE) for the web app, API, worker, MCP
server, and permissions; Cloud SQL (Postgres with pgvector); Secret Manager (references only); Vertex AI (models in the
same region); logging and monitoring; the company's identity provider. Cloud sources are read through an allowlist;
sources in the company's data centers (the EDW, Control-M) through a runner inside their network that only calls out.
Employees reach it with single sign-on. Nothing goes to an outside vendor.

## 10. How we build it

Foundations first: **Phase 0** (production fixes) → **Foundation** (knowledge core, learning loop, question sets, the
synthetic company, the Atlas, pointer mode) → **Capture** (Teams, Slack, meetings, handovers) → **Surfaces** (screens,
Teams and Slack bots, the frontline view) → **Packs** (onboarding, incidents, policies, contact center, merchandising,
supply chain, recalls, engineering and migration, leaders). Each phase is proven on a synthetic company with known
right answers before anything touches real data. Already built: documents, permissions, search and Ask, connectors for
Confluence, Jira, Drive, GitHub, Slack, Microsoft 365, Notion, catalogs, and directories, each syncing only what
changed since its last sync (a full reconcile daily) and surviving a source's rate limits; the Map; the Terraform
provider; Kubernetes manifests. Phase 0 so far: C1, P1 to P3, H1, H2, H4, H5, and H6. A connector only adds raw material; the core turns it into knowledge, keeps it fresh,
and learns from people, so every new connector and pack makes it smarter.
